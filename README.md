# Fooplan

Fooplan plans lunch and dinner for the next few days from a set of built-in recipes and the user's own, then turns the plan into a grocery list.

The interface is in English or German: a switch in the header ("EN" / "DE") changes it at once, the choice is saved in the browser, and a browser set to German starts in German. Recipe names, ingredients and steps are shown in German too; the user's own recipes stay as typed. Saved plans and recipe links use the English names in both languages.

The app opens on a home page (unknown URLs lead there too), which the "Fooplan" name in the header links to from every page. It says what the app does and offers one main button that follows the saved plan: "Start planning" while no meal is filled, "Continue planning · 3 of 10 meals picked" once some are, "Accept your plan" once every meal is filled, all three leading to the Plan page, and "Open the grocery list · 12 of 31 in the cart" once the plan is accepted, leading to the grocery list (its lines and added items, and how many of them are ticked). A "Today" panel, with the accent border of today's day card, shows today's date and its lunch and dinner as on the Plan page: the recipe linked to its page with its minutes (none for the user's own recipes), "Leftovers: <recipe>", "Eating out", or "Not picked yet"; when the plan has no day for today it says "Your plan doesn't include today." with a "Go to the plan" link. Three cards lead to the Plan page ("No meals picked yet", "3 of 10 meals picked", or once accepted the plan's summary, "5 days for 2 people"), the grocery list ("Ready once the plan is accepted", then "12 of 31 in the cart") and the Recipes page ("46 recipes, 2 of your own", counting the built-in and the user's own recipes). "How it works" ends the page with the three steps: pick the meals, accept the plan, shop the list. Leftovers and eating out count as picked meals throughout.

The "Plan" page plans lunch and dinner for 1–5 days starting today (default 5) and for a number of people (default 2). Days and People are steppers, not fields: a down and an up arrow button lower or raise the value by 1, and with the value focused ArrowDown and ArrowUp do the same; an arrow is disabled at its limit (Days 1 and 5, People 1, with no upper limit), and when the focused arrow becomes disabled the focus moves to the value. A "Kid-friendly" checkbox next to them keeps strong flavours out of the suggestions (chili, curry paste, fish sauce, anchovy, tamarind): turning it on replaces the open meals' suggestions that aren't kid-friendly, picked meals stay, and "×" then stops when 15 kid-friendly recipes are left; it's kept by "New plan" while at least 15 kid-friendly recipes are suggested, and an accepted plan says "N days for M people, kid-friendly". While it is off and fewer than 15 kid-friendly recipes are suggested, it is disabled, with a note below the toolbar saying why. More days adds open days at the end, fewer days drops them from the end. An accepted plan shows "N days for M people" instead. Today's day card is highlighted with an accent border and the cards of the days before today are greyed out, until hovered or focused; the marking follows the date when the page opens or a new plan starts. Each open meal offers three recipe suggestions: pick one, or ask for other suggestions. Suggestions aren't random: among the recipes not shown yet, the app prefers ones that differ in their base (pasta, rice, potato, bread) and main ingredient (chicken, meat, fish) from the day's other meal and the neighbouring days once those have a recipe (picked, or leftovers), that share ingredients with meals already picked, and that fit the meal (quick ones for lunch, longer ones for dinner, the longest for Friday to Sunday dinners); the three shown differ from each other the same way, and equally good candidates are drawn at random. Or choose "Eat out": the meal then shows "Eating out", counts as filled, adds nothing to the grocery list, and "Undo" opens it again with the suggestions it showed. Every suggestion and every picked meal shows how long its recipe takes to cook, in minutes ("35 minutes"); a leftovers lunch and the user's own recipes show no time. A small "Recipe" link next to each suggestion opens that recipe's page without picking it. "Undo" on a picked meal opens it again with the three suggestions it showed when picked, in the same order, also after a reload; a suggestion picked for another meal since, or no longer suggested, is replaced by another unpicked recipe, drawn as for any suggestion, while one another open meal is showing comes back as it is. A meal picked in a plan saved before picks remembered their suggestions gets three new ones instead, and so does one whose remembered suggestions held a recipe deleted since. A picked dinner (except on the last day) offers "Cook extra for tomorrow's lunch": the next day's lunch then becomes "Leftovers: <recipe>", whether it was open, picked or eating out (a lunch pick is dropped; its recipe can be suggested again only once the recipes not shown yet run out); it counts as filled and isn't a repeat, and the grocery list counts that dinner for twice the people. Unticking it, or undoing the dinner, opens that lunch again; shrinking the plan so that the dinner is on the last day turns the option off. No recipe is used twice in a plan: a picked recipe is never suggested for another meal. Each recipe is also suggested only once per plan: once it has appeared for any meal, no meal suggests it again, nor after its pick is undone; only "Undo" brings it back, to the meal that showed it. When the recipes not shown yet run out, a meal still gets three suggestions, taking the unpicked recipes shown longest ago first (an undone or dropped pick counts as shown just now), never one it is showing at that moment, and one another open meal is showing only when no other is left. "New plan" starts with no recipe shown. Once every meal is filled (a recipe, leftovers or eating out), "Accept plan" makes the plan read-only; such a dinner then shows "Cooking extra for tomorrow's lunch". Where the button was, the page then says "Plan accepted. Your grocery list is ready." with a "Grocery list" link to the grocery list, which gets the focus; the line and the link stay as long as the plan is accepted. "New plan", offered while planning and once accepted, starts an empty 5-day plan from today for the same number of people. When a meal is filled or the plan is accepted, it first asks in a dialog what's lost: the plan's dates and how many meals are filled ("7 of 10 meals picked", leftovers and eating out included), and whether the plan is accepted and grocery list ticks or added items will go; "Keep this plan", Escape or a click outside the dialog keeps the plan, "Start new plan" discards it, and the focus then goes back to "New plan". Without a filled meal or acceptance, the new plan starts right away. The recipe name of a picked meal, and the one in "Leftovers: <recipe>", links to that recipe's page, also once the plan is accepted. The plan is saved in the browser (`localStorage`), so it survives a reload and keeps its dates when reopened on a later day, and a trip to a recipe page leaves it as it was, apart from a recipe taken out of the suggestions or deleted there, and, while kid-friendly is on, an own recipe edited there to use a strong flavour, which is replaced in the open meals' suggestions. With fewer than 15 kid-friendly recipes suggested after such an edit, or when the plan opens (some taken out in another tab, say), kid-friendly goes off instead, unless the plan is accepted. "New plan" is the way to start a fresh one from today.

The "Grocery list" page lists what to buy for the accepted plan; until the plan is accepted it only says to accept a plan first, with a link to the plan. Each recipe's amounts are scaled from its servings to the number of people (a dinner cooked extra counts twice the people, and the lunch its leftovers cover adds nothing). The same ingredient in the same unit is merged into one line with the summed amount, and only then rounded up to a whole number (two half onions make one onion, 133.3 g becomes 134 g); the same ingredient in different units stays on separate lines. A total of 5 or 10 ml then reads as 1 or 2 tsp, and one of 15, 30 or 45 ml as 1, 2 or 3 tbsp; every other ml total stays in ml. Olive oil, sesame oil, vegetable oil, salt, pepper and black pepper are left off. The lines are grouped by shop section in the order Fruit & vegetables, Meat & fish, Dairy & eggs, Bread & bakery, Pasta, rice & grains, Cans & jars, Spices & sauces, Nuts & seeds, Drinks and Other (for an ingredient without a section), alphabetical by name and then unit within a section; sections without lines aren't shown. Ticking a line moves it out of its section into a collapsed "In the cart (n)" section at the bottom (alphabetical), where unticking it moves it back. A line leaving a list fades out over 150 ms (not when the system asks for reduced motion) while the count and the rest of the page change at once. When ticked or unticked with the keyboard, the focus then goes to the next line in the same section or cart, else to the previous one; after the last line of a section, to the first line of the next section, else to the cart; after the last line of the cart, to the unticked line back in its section. A click or tap leaves the focus alone. After a tick, a bar at the bottom of the screen says `"1 pc onion" is in the cart.` with an "Undo" button for 5 seconds (longer while the pointer or the focus is on it); "Undo" moves the line back to its section. Above the list, an "Add an item" field with an "Add" button (Enter adds too) adds the user's own items, such as "toilet paper" or "2 l milk": the text is added as typed, with surrounding spaces trimmed; a blank entry adds nothing, and neither does text already added, ignoring case, while it's still on the list. The field is empty again afterwards, with the focus in it. A line below the field then says what happened, and screen readers announce it: `Added "toilet paper" to Other.`, `"coffee" is already on the list.`, or `"coffee" is already in the cart.` when that item is ticked; a blank entry empties the line. An added item whose whole text is an ingredient name ignoring case ("Lemon") goes under that ingredient's section, any other (pantry staples such as "olive oil" too) under "Other"; in German a German ingredient name ("Zitrone") counts too, as do the English names, while in English a German name that differs from the English one goes under "Other"; it reads as typed, sorts by name among the other lines, after a plan line of the same name, and is never merged with one. Added items tick into the cart like the others, and only they have a "Remove" button, in their section and in the cart; when removing with the keyboard, the focus goes to the next line, else to the previous one, else to the field. Ticks and added items are saved with the plan, so they survive a reload; "New plan" clears them.

The "Recipes" page shows the 44 sample recipes from `src/app/recipes/recipes.json` with their servings and ingredients. Every built-in recipe takes at most 30 minutes of work and 45 minutes in total. Ingredient amounts of 5 or 10 ml read as 1 or 2 tsp, and of 15, 30 or 45 ml as 1, 2 or 3 tbsp (15 ml vegetable oil reads "1 tbsp vegetable oil"); every other ml amount, like 250 ml beef stock or 700 ml milk, stays in ml. A search field above them narrows the list while typing to the recipes whose name, or the name of one of their ingredients, contains the search text, ignoring case, accents and surrounding spaces, in their usual order; a line below the field then says how many recipes match (`5 recipes match "spaghetti".`, `1 recipe matches "carbonara".`), and when none matches it says "No recipe matches" instead of listing any, both announced by screen readers. An empty search shows all recipes. Each recipe's name links to its recipe page.

"Add a recipe" on the Recipes page opens a form for the user's own recipe: a name, servings from 1 to 12 and up to 30 ingredients, each with an amount, a unit and a name. Own recipes have no steps and no cooking time. They're listed after the built-in recipes, marked "Your recipe", have their own recipe page, are suggested and picked on the Plan page like the others, and their ingredients go on the grocery list. They're saved in the browser and kept by "New plan". Saving opens the new recipe's page, whose "Back" leads to the Recipes page. An own recipe's page has "Edit", which opens the form filled in, and "Delete", which asks first; a recipe picked in the plan can't be deleted until its pick is undone or a new plan is started, nor one whose deletion would leave fewer than 15 suggested recipes. While kid-friendly is on, this limit and the one on "Don't suggest" count kid-friendly recipes: once only 15 kid-friendly recipes are suggested, no suggested recipe can be deleted or taken out of the suggestions.

Every recipe has its own page at `/recipes/<slug>`, where the slug is its name without accents, lowercased, with every character but letters, digits, spaces and hyphens dropped and the words joined by single hyphens (`/recipes/nicoise-salad`, `/recipes/oven-baked-salmon-with-potatoes-and-green-beans`). It shows the recipe's name, its servings and its ingredients, with amounts as on the Recipes page. Below the ingredients, a built-in recipe's cooking steps are shown one at a time by default: "Step 3 of 8", that step's text, in which each ingredient's amount follows its first mention in parentheses ("slice the garlic cloves (2 pc)"; in German after its German name, "Die Knoblauchzehen (2 Stk.)"), and "Previous" and "Next" buttons. The page opens on step 1, where "Previous" is disabled; on the last step "Next" becomes "Done", which leaves the page like "Back". Another recipe opened on the page starts at its step 1. A switch above the steps, "Step by step" or "All steps", shows them one at a time or all at once as a numbered list; the choice is saved in the browser for every recipe page, and switching back shows the step shown before. Screen readers announce the counter and the step text after each "Previous" or "Next"; when "Previous" becomes disabled under the focus, the focus moves to "Next", and when "Done" replaces the focused "Next", to "Done". A "Back" link returns to the page the user came from inside the app, or to the Recipes page when the recipe page was opened directly, reloaded or reached from the add or edit form. A slug no recipe has, one in another case included, leads to the Recipes page, replacing the URL.

Recipes the user doesn't want can be taken out of the suggestions: with the "×" next to a suggestion on the Plan page, which asks first ("Don't suggest Shakshuka with bread?") and then puts another recipe in its place, or with "Don't suggest" on its card on the Recipes page or on its recipe page. Such a recipe is never suggested again, but stays on the Recipes page and its recipe page, marked "Not suggested", where "Suggest again" brings it back. Picks and accepted plans don't change. At least 15 recipes always stay suggested. The choice is saved in the browser and kept by "New plan".

## Running it

Needs Node ≥ 24.15.

```bash
npm install
npm start
```

Then open `http://localhost:4200/`.

## Testing and building

```bash
npx ng test --watch=false   # Vitest unit and component tests
npm run build               # type check and production build into dist/fooplan/
```

## Deployment

Every push to `main` runs the tests, builds the app and publishes it to GitHub Pages at <https://mihai238.github.io/fooplan/> (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`). A failing test stops the deployment. The workflow can also be started by hand from the Actions tab.

One-time setup: Settings → Pages → Source: "GitHub Actions".
