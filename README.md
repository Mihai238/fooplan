# Fooplan

Fooplan plans lunch and dinner for the next few days from a set of built-in recipes and the user's own, then turns the plan into a grocery list.

The app opens on the "Plan" page (unknown URLs lead there too). It plans lunch and dinner for 1–5 days starting today (default 5) and for a number of people (default 2). Days and People are steppers, not fields: a down and an up arrow button lower or raise the value by 1, and with the value focused ArrowDown and ArrowUp do the same; an arrow is disabled at its limit (Days 1 and 5, People 1, with no upper limit), and when the focused arrow becomes disabled the focus moves to the value. More days adds open days at the end, fewer days drops them from the end. An accepted plan shows "N days for M people" instead. Each open meal offers three recipe suggestions: pick one, or ask for other suggestions. Every suggestion and every picked meal shows how long its recipe takes to cook, in minutes ("35 minutes"); a leftovers lunch and the user's own recipes show no time. A small "Recipe" link next to each suggestion opens that recipe's page without picking it. "Undo" on a picked meal opens it again with the three suggestions it showed when picked, in the same order, also after a reload; a suggestion picked for another meal since, or no longer suggested, is replaced by another unpicked recipe, drawn as for any suggestion, while one another open meal is showing comes back as it is. A meal picked in a plan saved before picks remembered their suggestions gets three new ones instead, and so does one whose remembered suggestions held a recipe deleted since. A picked dinner (except on the last day) offers "Cook extra for tomorrow's lunch": the next day's lunch then becomes "Leftovers: <recipe>", whether it was open or picked (a lunch pick is dropped; its recipe can be suggested again only once the recipes not shown yet run out); it counts as filled and isn't a repeat, and the grocery list counts that dinner for twice the people. Unticking it, or undoing the dinner, opens that lunch again; shrinking the plan so that the dinner is on the last day turns the option off. No recipe is used twice in a plan: a picked recipe is never suggested for another meal. Each recipe is also suggested only once per plan: once it has appeared for any meal, no meal suggests it again, nor after its pick is undone; only "Undo" brings it back, to the meal that showed it. When the recipes not shown yet run out, a meal still gets three suggestions, taking the unpicked recipes shown longest ago first (an undone or dropped pick counts as shown just now), never one it is showing at that moment, and one another open meal is showing only when no other is left. "New plan" starts with no recipe shown. Once every meal is filled (a recipe or leftovers), "Accept plan" makes the plan read-only; such a dinner then shows "Cooking extra for tomorrow's lunch". "New plan", offered while planning and once accepted, starts an empty 5-day plan from today for the same number of people. When a meal is filled or the plan is accepted, it first asks in a dialog what's lost: the plan's dates and how many meals are filled ("7 of 10 meals picked", leftovers included), and whether the plan is accepted and grocery list ticks or added items will go; "Keep this plan", Escape or a click outside the dialog keeps the plan, "Start new plan" discards it, and the focus then goes back to "New plan". Without a filled meal or acceptance, the new plan starts right away. The recipe name of a picked meal, and the one in "Leftovers: <recipe>", links to that recipe's page, also once the plan is accepted. The plan is saved in the browser (`localStorage`), so it survives a reload and keeps its dates when reopened on a later day, and a trip to a recipe page leaves it as it was, apart from a recipe taken out of the suggestions or deleted there; "New plan" is the way to start a fresh one from today.

The "Grocery list" page lists what to buy for the accepted plan; until the plan is accepted it only says to accept a plan first, with a link to the plan. Each recipe's amounts are scaled from its servings to the number of people (a dinner cooked extra counts twice the people, and the lunch its leftovers cover adds nothing). The same ingredient in the same unit is merged into one line with the summed amount, and only then rounded up to a whole number (two half onions make one onion, 133.3 g becomes 134 g); the same ingredient in different units stays on separate lines. A total of 5 or 10 ml then reads as 1 or 2 tsp, and one of 15, 30 or 45 ml as 1, 2 or 3 tbsp; every other ml total stays in ml. Olive oil, sesame oil, vegetable oil, salt, pepper and black pepper are left off. The lines are grouped by shop section in the order Fruit & vegetables, Meat & fish, Dairy & eggs, Bread & bakery, Pasta, rice & grains, Cans & jars, Spices & sauces, Nuts & seeds, Drinks and Other (for an ingredient without a section), alphabetical by name and then unit within a section; sections without lines aren't shown. Ticking a line moves it out of its section into a collapsed "In the cart (n)" section at the bottom (alphabetical), where unticking it moves it back. When ticked or unticked with the keyboard, the focus then goes to the next line in the same section or cart, else to the previous one; after the last line of a section, to the first line of the next section, else to the cart; after the last line of the cart, to the unticked line back in its section. A click or tap leaves the focus alone. Above the list, an "Add an item" field with an "Add" button (Enter adds too) adds the user's own items, such as "toilet paper" or "2 l milk": the text is added as typed, with surrounding spaces trimmed; a blank entry adds nothing, and neither does text already added, ignoring case, while it's still on the list. The field is empty again afterwards, with the focus in it. A line below the field then says what happened, and screen readers announce it: `Added "toilet paper" to Other.`, `"coffee" is already on the list.`, or `"coffee" is already in the cart.` when that item is ticked; a blank entry empties the line. An added item whose whole text is an ingredient name ignoring case ("Lemon") goes under that ingredient's section, any other (pantry staples such as "olive oil" too) under "Other"; it reads as typed, sorts by name among the other lines, after a plan line of the same name, and is never merged with one. Added items tick into the cart like the others, and only they have a "Remove" button, in their section and in the cart; when removing with the keyboard, the focus goes to the next line, else to the previous one, else to the field. Ticks and added items are saved with the plan, so they survive a reload; "New plan" clears them.

The "Recipes" page shows the 29 sample recipes from `src/app/recipes/recipes.json` with their servings and ingredients. Every built-in recipe takes at most 30 minutes of work and 45 minutes in total. Ingredient amounts of 5 or 10 ml read as 1 or 2 tsp, and of 15, 30 or 45 ml as 1, 2 or 3 tbsp (15 ml vegetable oil reads "1 tbsp vegetable oil"); every other ml amount, like 250 ml beef stock or 700 ml milk, stays in ml. A search field above them narrows the list while typing to the recipes whose name, or the name of one of their ingredients, contains the search text, ignoring case, accents and surrounding spaces, in their usual order; a line below the field then says how many recipes match (`3 recipes match "spaghetti".`, `1 recipe matches "carbonara".`), and when none matches it says "No recipe matches" instead of listing any, both announced by screen readers. An empty search shows all recipes. Each recipe's name links to its recipe page.

"Add a recipe" on the Recipes page opens a form for the user's own recipe: a name, servings from 1 to 12 and up to 30 ingredients, each with an amount, a unit and a name. Own recipes have no steps and no cooking time. They're listed after the built-in recipes, marked "Your recipe", have their own recipe page, are suggested and picked on the Plan page like the others, and their ingredients go on the grocery list. They're saved in the browser and kept by "New plan". Saving opens the new recipe's page, whose "Back" leads to the Recipes page. An own recipe's page has "Edit", which opens the form filled in, and "Delete", which asks first; a recipe picked in the plan can't be deleted until its pick is undone or a new plan is started, nor one whose deletion would leave fewer than 15 suggested recipes.

Every recipe has its own page at `/recipes/<slug>`, where the slug is its name without accents, lowercased, with every character but letters, digits, spaces and hyphens dropped and the words joined by single hyphens (`/recipes/nicoise-salad`, `/recipes/oven-baked-salmon-with-potatoes-and-green-beans`). It shows the recipe's name, its servings and its ingredients, with amounts as on the Recipes page. Below the ingredients, a built-in recipe's cooking steps are shown one at a time by default: "Step 3 of 8", that step's text, and "Previous" and "Next" buttons. The page opens on step 1, where "Previous" is disabled; on the last step "Next" becomes "Done", which leaves the page like "Back". Another recipe opened on the page starts at its step 1. A switch above the steps, "Step by step" or "All steps", shows them one at a time or all at once as a numbered list; the choice is saved in the browser for every recipe page, and switching back shows the step shown before. Screen readers announce the counter and the step text after each "Previous" or "Next"; when "Previous" becomes disabled under the focus, the focus moves to "Next", and when "Done" replaces the focused "Next", to "Done". A "Back" link returns to the page the user came from inside the app, or to the Recipes page when the recipe page was opened directly, reloaded or reached from the add or edit form. A slug no recipe has, one in another case included, leads to the Recipes page, replacing the URL.

Recipes the user doesn't want can be taken out of the suggestions: with the "×" next to a suggestion on the Plan page, which puts another recipe in its place, or with "Don't suggest" on its card on the Recipes page or on its recipe page. Such a recipe is never suggested again, but stays on the Recipes page and its recipe page, marked "Not suggested", where "Suggest again" brings it back. Picks and accepted plans don't change. At least 15 recipes always stay suggested. The choice is saved in the browser and kept by "New plan".

## Running it

Needs Node ≥ 24.15.

```bash
npm install
npm start
```

Then open `http://localhost:4200/`.

## Testing and building

```bash
npx ng test --watch=false   # Vitest unit and component tests
npm run build               # type check and production build into dist/fooplan/
```

## Deployment

Every push to `main` runs the tests, builds the app and publishes it to GitHub Pages at <https://mihai238.github.io/fooplan/> (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`). A failing test stops the deployment. The workflow can also be started by hand from the Actions tab.

One-time setup: Settings → Pages → Source: "GitHub Actions".
